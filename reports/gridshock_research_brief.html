Scope and metric definitions keep the comparison auditable

  • Product: hourly DE-LU day-ahead auction price in EUR/MWh for 1 January–30 September 2025; the later 15-minute market regime is excluded.
  • Decision cutoff: 12:00 Europe/Berlin on D-1. Predictive weather fields use Open-Meteo previous_day2, approximately 48 hours before validity.
  • Weather geography: equal-weight observations for Berlin, Hamburg, Frankfurt, and Munich; this is explicitly not labelled a load-weighted national index.
  • Evaluation: expanding rolling-origin folds, with the final 45 delivery days held out once for publication.
  • Sources: Fraunhofer ISE Energy-Charts / SMARD and Open-Meteo Previous Runs API; 45 bounded requests are hashed in the fixture manifest.

Model specification and validation design

The comparators are a 48-hour price-lag Seasonal naive forecast, a median-imputed and standardised ridge regression, and a bounded histogram gradient-boosting regressor. Every transformer is fitted inside its training fold. The feature ledger rejects any row whose maximum source availability is later than the simulated cutoff.

Reported forecast metrics are MAE, RMSE, median absolute error, and mean within-day Spearman rank correlation. Strategy accounting uses the HGB holdout predictions only, charges cost on every non-zero leg, and includes a shuffled-prediction placebo.

Limitations and robustness

  • Historical association and predictive accuracy do not establish a causal weather-price relationship.
  • The weather archive changes as numerical models evolve; the report therefore limits itself to a documented common-coverage window.
  • The ranking proxy omits bids, fills, liquidity, fees beyond the declared per-leg cost, imbalance risk, collateral, and physical delivery.
  • A 45-day holdout is informative but too short to establish persistence across years or market-design regimes.
  • The 15-minute Single Day-Ahead Coupling regime beginning 1 October 2025 is a different product and is intentionally excluded.
